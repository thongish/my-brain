# What is a business process?
- A **business process** is a series of interrelated tasks or activities performed to achieve a specific organizational goal. These processes can involve multiple stakeholders, systems, and departments. Business processes can be as broad as the tasks that happen as soon as a customer walks into Starbucks until the customer receives their order and leaves, or as small as the tasks related to just taking the customer's order.
# What is business process management(BPM)?
- **Business Process Management (BPM)** is a systematic approach to analyzing, designing, executing, monitoring, and optimizing business processes. The goal of BPM is to improve efficiency, reduce costs, ensure compliance, and enhance organizational agility. It often involves the use of specialized tools and software to model and automate workflows. 
# What is a "Business Process Model"?
- A **business process model** is a visual representation of a business process. It typically depicts the sequence of activities, decision points, inputs, outputs, and roles involved in a process. This model helps stakeholders understand and analyze the process flow for improvements. An example of this would be a **Cross-Functional Flow Diagram** (Swimlane Diagram), which is, in general, a linear visualization of a process from start to end. 
# What are the benefits of documenting and analyzing business process models?
- Documenting and analyzing business process models provide several benefits:
	- **Improved Understanding**: Provides a clear picture of how a process works, making it easier to train new employees by letting them know how their work contributes to the overall process.
	- **Identifying Inefficiencies**: Highlights bottlenecks, redundancies, and areas for improvement.
	- **Enhanced Communication**: Facilitates better communication between teams and departments. Business process models break down how various roles and systems interact, which can promote collaboration and reduce misunderstandings.
	- **Standardization**: Helps ensure consistency and compliance with organizational or regulatory standards. This leads to more predictable outcomes and improved quality of process outputs.
	- **Process Optimization**: With a clear view of a process, organizations can redesign workflows to make them more efficient. This could involve automation of tasks, reallocating resources, or adopting new technologies.
	- **Risk Mitigation**: Helps identify and address potential risks or vulnerabilities, such as compliance issues. An example would be identifying which tasks where errors are likely to occur or points where sensitive data could be compromised. 
# What label is given to each swim lane?
- Each "lane" in a Swimlane Diagram is labeled with the name of a role, department, or system responsible for performing the tasks within that lane. Examples could be "Customer Support", "Finance", or "Order Management System"
# What is the naming convention for each process step?
- Each **process step** should be named using a clear an descriptive action verb followed by a noun to indicate the specific task being performed. This ensures that the process steps are concise and easily understood.
- For example:
	- Submit Application
	- Approve Payment
	- Verify Identity
# What is the "Happy Path"?
- The **Happy Path** refers to the sequence of steps in a process where everything proceeds as expected, without errors, exceptions, or deviations. It represents the ideal scenario where all conditions are met, and the process flows smoothly from start to finish. Understanding the Happy Path is crucial for identifying and managing alternative paths or exceptions.
